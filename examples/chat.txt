Yes. India has a specific law that prohibits discrimination against people living with HIV.
The law is the Human Immunodeficiency Virus and Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome
(Prevention and Control) Act, 2017, commonly called the HIV/AIDS Act, 2017. It came into
force in 2018. [Image]India Code+1
What discrimination is prohibited?
A person cannot be treated unfairly merely because they are HIV-positive in areas such as:
Employment (hiring, promotion, termination)
Education (school, college admissions)
Healthcare and hospital treatment
Housing and renting property
Insurance services
Access to public facilities and government services
Holding public or private office positions

HIV medicines (called antiretroviral therapy (ART)) can cause side effects, including skin rashes,
but most people tolerate modern HIV medications quite well.
Can HIV medicines cause rashes?
Yes. Some ART drugs can cause:
Mild red rash
Itching
Small raised bumps on the skin
In many cases, a mild rash appears within the first few weeks of starting treatment and then
improves.
Are the medicines "very strong"?
Modern HIV medicines are much safer and better tolerated than older treatments. Most people
can take them for many years with few or no significant side effects. In fact, effective ART allows
many people with HIV to live nearly normal lifespans.
When is a rash serious?
Seek medical attention promptly if a rash is accompanied by:
Fever
Blisters
Skin peeling
Mouth sores
Eye redness
Facial swelling
Difficulty breathing
These can indicate a severe allergic reaction and may require changing medications.
